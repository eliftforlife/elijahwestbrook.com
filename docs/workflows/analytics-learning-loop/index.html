Weekly Analytics and Learning Loop

Every week, your numbers from social, ads, website and sales are pulled into one report that says what did well, what did badly and why, and writes next week's changes (it makes the safe ones itself and asks you about the rest).

Set upAbout 3 to 5 hours the first time (most of it is giving read access to each platform). Then 15 to 20 minutes a week.DifficultymediumFrom14 videos
Untested means

This workflow is built from what the videos in our study show working. We haven’t run it on our own business yet, so it’s free. When we’ve tested it, the tested version (with our results and fixes) becomes paid. General information, not legal, tax or financial advice: check the rules where you operate.

What it covers

Track what performs (analytics), Build reports and dashboards, Report on ad results, Adjust budgets and pause losing ads.

Doesn’t cover: It does not make the content, run the ads, answer customers or post anything. It does not spend money, change ad budgets or pause ads without your yes. It cannot read a platform you have not given it access to, and it cannot tell you WHY something worked when the sample is tiny.

1. Start with the interview

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT. It asks you questions one at a time, then writes a profile of your business that every later step uses.

Interview prompt
You are setting up an automation called **Weekly Analytics and Learning Loop** for my business. Before building anything, interview me so the automation fits my business exactly.

How to interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Keep each question short and plain.
- When I'm vague, ask a follow-up with 2-4 example answers I can pick from.
- When I say "skip" or "not sure", use the safest default, mark it `(default)`, and move on.
- Never ask me for a password, card number, API key or code. When a login or key is needed, write down where it will be needed and that I'll add it myself.
- About every 6 questions, tell me how many are left.
- Stop after at most 25 questions, or sooner once you have what you need.

Cover these, in this order:
1. **The business.** What it sells, to whom, where, and how big it is (people, rough monthly customers or sales).
2. **The goal.** What this automation should achieve in 30 days, and the one number that proves it worked.
3. **Today.** Who does this job now, how, how long it takes a week, and what goes wrong.
4. **Voice.** How we talk to customers. Ask for 2-3 real examples (a post, an email, a reply) and words or claims we never use.
5. **Tools and accounts.** Which apps I use for email, calendar, CRM, payments, social, website and files. Who owns each login. Whether 2-factor codes go to my phone.
6. **Rules.** Things that must always or never happen: legal, safety, refunds, prices, medical or financial claims, and when a human must step in.
7. **Approvals.** What can go out on its own from day 1, and what I want to approve first (and for how long).
8. **Data.** Where the facts come from (price list, FAQ, product data, past posts), and where results should be saved.
9. **Measuring.** Where the numbers live (analytics, CRM, bank) and whether I can give read access.
10. **Schedule and budget.** When it should run, quiet hours, and the monthly tool budget.
11. **WORKFLOW QUESTIONS:** 
   - Which places do you post or sell (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, email, website, shop, ads)? List every one, even small.
   - For each place, what is the ONE number you care about most (sales, leads, watch time, signups, bookings)?
   - Where do you look at the numbers today, and can you give read-only access (a viewer login, an export, or a connected app)?
   - How long after posting do you judge a post? (We suggest checkpoints at 48 hours and 7 days. Do you want different ones?)
   - What counts as a win and what counts as a miss for you? Give a real example of each.
   - Do you run paid ads? Which platform, what monthly spend, and what is the most you would ever let a tool change without asking you?
   - Which things should the AI be allowed to change by itself (next week's topics, post times, lengths, hooks) and which must ask you first (budgets, pricing, offers, anything public)?
   - Which day and time should the report land, and where (email, Slack, a doc, a Google Sheet)?
   - What have you already tried that did not work, so it does not suggest it again?
   - Who gets the report besides you (partner, team, VA)?

When done, write `profile.md` with these headings, filled from my answers:
`Business` · `Goal and success number` · `Current process and pain` · `Voice (with examples)` · `Never say or do` · `Tools and who owns each login` · `Needs human approval` · `Facts and data sources` · `Where results go` · `Analytics access` · `Schedule` · `Budget` · `Workflow settings` · `Open questions`.
Then list the setup steps only I can do (logins, keys, payments), in order, each with a time estimate.

Models

  • Pulling and cleaning numbers: A cheap, fast model (a small Claude or OpenAI model) or no model at all, just the connector. This is copy work. Do not pay for a strong model to move numbers.
  • Comparing and explaining what did well or badly: The strongest model you have (Claude Opus or Sonnet class, or the best GPT). Judgment and caution about small samples matter here.
  • Writing next week's plan and the owner summary: A strong model. Needs to follow your profile.md rules and keep the voice plain.
  • Checking the report against the raw numbers: A second, different model if you can. A second opinion catches maths and made-up causes.

4. How it runs

  1. Wake upScheduler (Claude scheduled task, n8n schedule, Make schedule)

    Runs every Monday at the time you chose. Also runs a small daily check that looks for posts that just hit 48 hours or 7 days old.

  2. Pull the numbersConnectors or APIs, or your exported CSVs

    Social: views, watch time or average view duration, click rate, follows, shares, comments per post. Ads: spend, impressions, clicks, cost per result, results. Site: visits, top pages, where visitors came from, signups. Sales: orders or bookings, revenue, refunds. Save a raw copy in the Scoreboard sheet with the date pulled.

  3. Clean and check the dataCheap model or script

    Fill gaps with 'missing' (never zero), remove duplicates, check that each source covers the same seven days, and flag any number that moved more than 3x from last week as 'check the source'.

  4. Judge each post at fixed checkpointsStrong model

    Compare each post only with your own posts of the same type and platform, at the same age (48 hours, then 7 days). Label each: beat the usual, normal, or under the usual. Do not judge before the checkpoint.

  5. Compare adsStrong model

    Rank ads by your chosen number (cost per result or click rate). Only judge an ad once it has enough data (default: 1,000 impressions or the spend you set). Mark each: keep, watch, or propose to pause. Never act on it.

  6. Connect the dotsStrong model

    Line up site visits and sales against posts and ads in the same week. Say what moved together. Write 'correlation, not proof' next to any link and say what a test would confirm it.

  7. Write the reportStrong model

    One page: the week in three lines, the 3 best things and why (with evidence), the 3 weakest and why (with evidence), numbers vs last week and vs your 4-week average, and what we still do not know.

  8. Check the reportSecond model or script

    Every number in the report is checked against the Scoreboard sheet. Every 'because' must point to a number or say 'guess'.

  9. Write next week's changesStrong model

    Two lists. Safe changes it makes itself in plan-next-week.md (topics to repeat, hooks to try, lengths, posting times). Proposals that need your yes (ad pauses, budget moves, offer or price changes, new platforms). At most 3 changes in total so you can tell what worked.

  10. Log what changedFile write

    Append to learnings.md: date, what was tried, what the numbers said, what changed, and which later checkpoint will test it.

  11. Deliver and wait for your yesEmail, Slack or doc

    Send the report with a clear 'Approve / Skip / Ask me' line for each proposal. Approved ad changes are listed for you or a team member to make; the loop itself only reads.

6. Quality checks

CheckIf it fails
Every number in the report matches the Scoreboard sheet (fact-check prompt returns PASS).Hold the report, regenerate once, and if it still fails send the raw sheet to the owner with a note.
All sources cover the same seven days and none are missing.Report anyway but put a clear 'Missing: [source]' line at the top. Never fill a gap with zero or a guess.
No post is judged before its 48-hour or 7-day mark, and none with fewer than 5 comparable posts.Label it 'too early' or 'too few to judge'.
No more than 3 changes are proposed, and none repeats a logged failure.Cut to the 3 with the strongest numbers behind them.
Nothing in LIST B was acted on by the AI.Stop the loop, undo the change, and turn off autonomous steps until you review the setup.
HOLD FOR A HUMAN: a number moved more than 3x, a refund or complaint spike appears, ad spend exceeds your cap, or a platform sign-in has expired.Send a short alert to the owner and skip the 'changes' step until they reply.

7. Learning loop

Reads: Social platform analytics (per post), ad platform results, website analytics, and sales or bookings. Pulled weekly (Monday) plus a daily check for posts that just reached 48 hours or 7 days.

When: Fixed checkpoints: 48 hours and 7 days per post, weekly for the whole business, and a monthly look at what the changes added up to. Never judge at random times.

Compares: Each post against your own posts of the same type, platform and age; this week against last week and against your 4-week average; ads against each other on one chosen number. Needs at least 5 comparable posts or the agreed ad data before it says anything.

Changes on its own: On its own: next week's content plan file (topics to repeat or drop, hooks, lengths, posting times, caption wording) and learnings.md. With your yes only: ad pauses and budget moves, prices, offers, new platforms. Max 3 changes a week so you can tell what worked.

Guardrails: Read-only access to every platform. It cannot spend money, post, delete or change an ad. It never judges small samples. It keeps a written undo for every change. A change that did not help after its checkpoint is logged as failed and not repeated. If results drop two weeks in a row after a change, it proposes undoing it. You can switch off the self-made changes at any time and keep the reports.

9. What goes wrong (and the fix)

ProblemFix
Platform sign-in or token expires and the pull silently returns nothing.The data check marks that source 'missing' and sends an alert. Reconnect it. Never let a blank become a zero.
Connector gives mixed or inconsistent results (a study recipe saw this with a campaign search).Add the exact account or URL to profile.md, and have the fact-check step compare with an export once a month.
The AI invents a reason for a result.The 'because' rule: each cause needs a number or the word 'guess'. The checker prompt flags the rest.
Choosing 'Always allow' on a connector lets the AI change live things (a study creator warned this can break a store).Keep connectors read-only or 'Ask for permission'. Make this loop read-only.
Too many changes at once, so you cannot tell what worked.Cap at 3 changes and log each with its own checkpoint.
Creative or posting volume falls behind spend and results drop (one ads recipe discussed this).The report shows posts or creatives per week next to spend, so a gap shows up.
Very big data sets are slow (a recipe capped one run at 3,000 reviews).Limit each pull to the last 7 days or the last N items.
The desktop app is closed when the scheduled task should run.Leave the app open on schedule, or move to n8n or Make for always-on.

10. Proof from the study

The study has 113 videos on 'Track what performs (analytics)': about 22% ran fully by themselves, 76% with a person in the loop, 2% needed a person. 'Build reports and dashboards': 218 videos, 36% fully automatic. 'Report on ad results': 10 videos, 50% automatic. 'Adjust budgets and pause losing ads': 29 videos, 45% fully automatic. Top tools across these tasks: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Google Sheets, Gmail, n8n, Make, Slack. None of the recipes we read had an automated learning loop beyond a periodic review (one compared before and after a store change); most were summaries with a person deciding, so the loop in this workflow is our own design.
