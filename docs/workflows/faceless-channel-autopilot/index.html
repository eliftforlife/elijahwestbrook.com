Faceless Channel Autopilot

A faceless short-video channel gets new videos made, checked, scheduled and reviewed against their results every day, with you approving only what the checks flag.

Set upAbout 4 to 8 hours the first time, spread over a weekend, plus a 1 to 2 week trial period with you approving every video.DifficultyhardFrom15 videos
Untested means

This workflow is built from what the videos in our study show working. We haven’t run it on our own business yet, so it’s free. When we’ve tested it, the tested version (with our results and fixes) becomes paid. General information, not legal, tax or financial advice: check the rules where you operate.

What it covers

Come up with content ideas and research trends, Write scripts and hooks, Record voiceovers, Make videos with an AI avatar or AI presenter, Edit videos, Make graphics, carousels and images, Add captions and subtitles, Make thumbnails and cover images, Write titles, descriptions and hashtags, Schedule and publish posts, Track what performs (analytics), Check work for quality and mistakes.

Doesn’t cover: Choosing the niche and the channel's rules, creating and verifying the accounts, paying for tools, copyright and platform-policy decisions, monetization setup, and replying to comments. It also doesn't guarantee views. No video in the study showed a fully hands-off channel with a proven result.

1. Start with the interview

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT. It asks you questions one at a time, then writes a profile of your business that every later step uses.

Interview prompt
You are setting up an automation called **Faceless Channel Autopilot** for my business. Before building anything, interview me so the automation fits my business exactly.

How to interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Keep each question short and plain.
- When I'm vague, ask a follow-up with 2-4 example answers I can pick from.
- When I say "skip" or "not sure", use the safest default, mark it `(default)`, and move on.
- Never ask me for a password, card number, API key or code. When a login or key is needed, write down where it will be needed and that I'll add it myself.
- About every 6 questions, tell me how many are left.
- Stop after at most 25 questions, or sooner once you have what you need.

Cover these, in this order:
1. **The business.** What it sells, to whom, where, and how big it is (people, rough monthly customers or sales).
2. **The goal.** What this automation should achieve in 30 days, and the one number that proves it worked.
3. **Today.** Who does this job now, how, how long it takes a week, and what goes wrong.
4. **Voice.** How we talk to customers. Ask for 2-3 real examples (a post, an email, a reply) and words or claims we never use.
5. **Tools and accounts.** Which apps I use for email, calendar, CRM, payments, social, website and files. Who owns each login. Whether 2-factor codes go to my phone.
6. **Rules.** Things that must always or never happen: legal, safety, refunds, prices, medical or financial claims, and when a human must step in.
7. **Approvals.** What can go out on its own from day 1, and what I want to approve first (and for how long).
8. **Data.** Where the facts come from (price list, FAQ, product data, past posts), and where results should be saved.
9. **Measuring.** Where the numbers live (analytics, CRM, bank) and whether I can give read access.
10. **Schedule and budget.** When it should run, quiet hours, and the monthly tool budget.
11. **WORKFLOW QUESTIONS:** 
   - What is the channel about, and who exactly watches it? Name the niche in one sentence.
   - Which platforms will it post to (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, others), and are the accounts already created and verified?
   - Do you already have channels or videos you like in this niche? Give 3 links or names to learn from.
   - What video length and how many videos per day or week do you want?
   - Which voice do you want: a stock AI voice, a clone of your own voice, or none? Do you have permission to use that voice?
   - What look should the visuals have: stock footage, AI images, animated images, gameplay, whiteboard, text only?
   - Which topics, people, brands or claims are off limits (medical, legal, financial, politics, kids, copyrighted characters)?
   - How will the channel earn: ads, creator rewards, affiliate links, or sending people to your business? What is the link or call to action, if any?
   - How many videos can you personally approve each day during week one, and do you want to keep approving after that?
   - What is your monthly budget for voice, image, video and posting tools, and what is your hard stop on spend?

When done, write `profile.md` with these headings, filled from my answers:
`Business` · `Goal and success number` · `Current process and pain` · `Voice (with examples)` · `Never say or do` · `Tools and who owns each login` · `Needs human approval` · `Facts and data sources` · `Where results go` · `Analytics access` · `Schedule` · `Budget` · `Workflow settings` · `Open questions`.
Then list the setup steps only I can do (logins, keys, payments), in order, each with a time estimate.

Models

  • Idea research and ranking: A strong reasoning model (Claude or ChatGPT). Judgment about what fits the niche and search demand matters more than speed.
  • Script writing and rewrite: A strong writing model (Claude Sonnet class or better). The hook and pacing decide retention; cheap models write flat scripts.
  • QA review of script and captions: A fresh session of a strong model. A second read catches what the writer missed.
  • Voiceover: ElevenLabs (used in 77 videos in the study), or a clone of your own voice. Most-used voice tool in the study. Generate per paragraph for better expression.
  • Images and video clips: An image model (Flux, Leonardo, Midjourney, GPT image) plus an image-to-video model (Kling, PixVerse, Higgsfield). Recipes used still images animated into 5 to 10 second clips.
  • Captions: Whisper-style transcription for word timing. Gives exact word timestamps for burned-in captions.
  • Labeling, tagging, sheet updates, weekly analytics math: A cheap, fast model. Simple tasks; saves cost.

4. How it runs

  1. Find ideasAI + trend source (YouTube search, Google Trends, reference channels)

    Reads profile.md and learnings.md, looks at your reference channels and trends, and adds 10 scored ideas to ideas.csv. Drops anything on the never-do list or already used.

  2. Pick today's topicsAI

    Chooses the top ideas for today's slots. Keeps about 80% proven topic types and 20% experiments (see learning loop).

  3. Write the scriptAI (strong writing model)

    Writes a script with a first-line hook, short sentences, one idea per beat, and an end. Saves to scripts/<id>.md with its tags (hook type, length, topic).

  4. Script checkAI (fresh session)

    Scores the hook, facts and policy risk. Rewrites once if the hook is weak. Holds the video for you if a fact cannot be verified.

  5. Make the voiceoverVoice tool (ElevenLabs or similar)

    Generates audio paragraph by paragraph, then joins it. Trims long pauses.

  6. Make visualsImage model + image-to-video model, or stock footage

    Splits the script into scenes, writes an image prompt per scene, generates images, animates them into short clips, and waits (polls) until every clip is finished.

  7. Assemble and captionEditor (CapCut, ffmpeg or an editing API)

    Lays clips over audio, burns in word-timed captions, adds music at low volume, exports vertical MP4.

  8. Write title, caption, hashtagsAI

    One search phrase per video, then different text for each platform. Saves to queue.csv.

  9. Quality checksScript (ffmpeg checks) + AI review

    Runs the checks in section 7. Passing videos get status 'ready'. Failing ones get fixed once, then held for you.

  10. Schedule and postPosting tool or platform schedulers

    Posts ready videos at the chosen times. Marks the row 'posted' with the link. Never re-posts a row already marked posted. In approve-everything mode it waits for your OK.

  11. Measure and learnPlatform analytics + AI

    At 48 hours and 7 days, pulls each video's numbers, compares to the channel's recent median, and writes lessons to learnings.md. Tomorrow's ideas and scripts read it.

{{profile}}

{{learnings}}

Ideas already used (titles):
{{used_ideas}}

{{trend_notes}}

For each give: working title, the single search phrase someone would type to find it, hook type (number, question, claim or story), target length in seconds, topic type, why it should work for THIS channel, and a risk flag (none, fact-check needed, policy risk). Skip anything on the never-do list. Return a CSV with those columns and nothing else.
Script writerStep 3. One run per chosen idea.
Write a voiceover script for one short vertical video.

Channel profile and voice rules:
{{profile}}

Idea:
{{idea_row}}

Rules:
- Target length: {{seconds}} seconds spoken aloud (about 2.5 words per second).
- The first sentence is the hook. It must make a viewer curious or surprised within 3 seconds. No greeting, no channel name.
- Short sentences. One idea. One fact or example per beat.
- Do not state a statistic, date or name unless I gave it to you in the idea row or notes. Mark anything uncertain with [VERIFY].
- End with a line that fits the call to action in the profile, or just end on the strongest point.
- Never use anything on the never-say list.

Return: the spoken script as plain paragraphs, then a list of scene beats (one line each, what we should SEE), then the tags: hook type, length, topic.
Script reviewer (fresh session)Step 4. Run in a new chat so it has no memory of writing the script.
You are a strict reviewer. You did not write this script.

Channel profile and rules:
{{profile}}

Script:
{{script}}

Score from 1 to 10: (a) hook strength in the first 3 seconds, (b) clarity and pacing, (c) sounds human, not like a template, (d) matches channel voice. Then check: any claim that could be false, any [VERIFY] tags, anything on the never-do list, anything that breaks YouTube, TikTok or Instagram policy for misleading, harmful or reused content.

Answer in JSON: {"scores":{...},"problems":[...],"rewrite_hook":"...","decision":"pass|fix|hold_for_human"}. Use hold_for_human for any unverifiable fact, any sensitive topic, or any score under 5.
Scene prompt makerStep 6. Turns scene beats into image and motion prompts.
Turn these scene beats into image prompts and motion prompts for an image-to-video model.

Visual style from the profile:
{{style}}

Scene beats:
{{beats}}

For each scene return: image_prompt (subject, setting, camera, lighting, style words from the profile, vertical 9:16), motion_prompt (one simple camera or subject move, 5 seconds), and on_screen_text (3 words or fewer, or none). Keep the same character and look across all scenes. Do not include real people, logos or copyrighted characters unless the profile allows it. Return JSON.
Title and caption writerStep 8. Per-platform text.
Write the posting text for this video.

Profile:
{{profile}}
Script:
{{script}}
Search phrase: {{search_phrase}}

Return JSON with keys youtube_title (under 70 characters, contains the search phrase, promises only what the video shows), youtube_description (2 sentences, then 3 hashtags), tiktok_caption (under 150 characters, casual), instagram_caption (2 short lines plus up to 5 hashtags), alt_text, and pinned_comment. Each platform's text must be different. No fake urgency, no guaranteed results.
Weekly learning reviewStep 11. Runs after the 7-day checkpoint.
You are the channel's analyst. Read posts.csv (every post with tags, post time, and the 48h and 7d numbers: views, average view duration or percent watched, swipe-away rate if available, likes, shares, followers gained).

For each tag (hook type, length, topic, post time) compare the median of posts with that tag to the median of all posts in the last 30 days. Only call something a pattern if at least 5 posts have that tag. List: what to do more of, what to do less of, and one experiment for next week (change ONE thing only). Do not change the niche, voice or rules in profile.md. Write 5 lines maximum to learnings.md, each with the date, the evidence (numbers and post count) and the change. If there are fewer than 10 posts, write 'not enough data' and change nothing.

Script review passes (average score 7 or higher, none under 5), no unresolved [VERIFY] tags.Rewrite once. If still failing, hold for human.

7. Learning loop

Reads: Per-video views, average view duration or percent watched, swipe-away rate, shares and followers gained, from each platform's analytics (or the posting tool), plus the video's tags saved at creation: hook type, length, topic, post time.

When: Fixed checkpoints per video: 48 hours and 7 days after posting. A weekly review on the same day each week looks across all posts.

Compares: Each video against the channel's own median for the last 30 days, and each tag group against the rest, on the same platform. It needs at least 5 posts per tag and 10 posts total before it draws a conclusion.

Changes on its own: On its own: which topic types and hook types get more of next week's slots, preferred length, posting times, and the hook wording in the prompts. It queues one experiment at a time.

Guardrails: Changes only one variable per week. At most 20% of slots go to experiments. It never changes the niche, voice, never-do list, spend limits or platforms. It never deletes posts. A video that gets a policy warning, copyright claim or a very high swipe-away rate pauses that topic type and messages you. Every change is written to learnings.md with the evidence, so you can undo it.

9. What goes wrong (and the fix)

ProblemFix
A scene looks worse than the rest of the video (common in the recipes).Regenerate only that scene; the QA step checks each clip.
Weak hook. The most common reason a short fails.The script reviewer rewrites the first line; the learning loop favors the hook types that win on your channel.
Free tools add watermarks or limit length (HeyGen, D-ID, PixVerse free plans).Use a paid plan or a tool without a watermark; QA fails any file with one.
Voiceover sounds flat when the whole script is generated at once.Generate per paragraph and join; trim pauses.
Voice or avatar clone sounds bad from a poor or short sample.Record a longer clean sample (one recipe advised at least 2 minutes of video for an avatar) or use a stock voice.
Video and audio lengths don't match, or an image/video model hasn't finished.Poll until every item says complete; match clip length to audio before assembly.
Posting fails from quotas, rate limits or an expired account connection.Check the posting tool's failed-posts list and reconnect the account. The run marks the row 'failed', not 'posted'.
New or unverified accounts block thumbnails or automation until phone verification is done.Verify every account by hand first.
Wrong audience setting (for example 'made for kids') cuts reach and features.Set it by hand at channel setup and re-check monthly.
Copyright, reused-content or AI-disclosure problems (one creator reported a strike).Use original or licensed media, follow each platform's AI-label rule, and hold anything borderline for human review.
Wrong niche or low-demand topics.Check a search phrase has real demand before scripting; the idea prompt asks for it.
Nothing learned from results. Almost no recipe had a closed loop.The 48h/7d review and learnings.md do it, with guardrails.

10. Proof from the study

Study task numbers (1,876 videos): ideas 435 videos (17% run fully alone), scripts 331 (13%), voiceover 187 (66%), AI avatars 199 (68%), video editing 332 (36%), graphics 286 (48%), thumbnails 104 (24%), titles and hashtags 133 (18%), scheduling posts 271 (73%), content analytics 113 (22%). Top tools: ChatGPT, Claude, CapCut, ElevenLabs, n8n. So voice and scheduling are usually automated, while ideas, scripts and learning from results usually still involve a human. 15 recipes were built from faceless-channel videos. Most were run by hand, one stage at a time, and almost none had an automatic learning loop. Income claims in those videos were not checked; do not rely on them.
