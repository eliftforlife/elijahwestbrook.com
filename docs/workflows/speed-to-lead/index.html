Speed to lead: reply to every new lead in under a minute, qualify and book them

Every new lead from a form, an ad or a DM gets a personal-sounding reply within a minute, is asked a few qualifying questions, is booked onto a call or followed up automatically, and is logged in your CRM, with you stepping in only for the ones that need a human.

Set upAbout 3 to 5 hours for one lead source and one channel (email or SMS). Add 1 to 2 hours for each extra source, such as Instagram DMs or an ad lead form.DifficultymediumFrom14 videos
Untested means

This workflow is built from what the videos in our study show working. We haven’t run it on our own business yet, so it’s free. When we’ve tested it, the tested version (with our results and fixes) becomes paid. General information, not legal, tax or financial advice: check the rules where you operate.

What it covers

Reply to new leads within minutes, Qualify leads, Book sales calls and appointments, Follow up with leads who went quiet, Log calls and update the CRM.

Doesn’t cover: Finding or buying leads, writing your ads, running the sales call itself, negotiating or closing, and cold outreach to people who never asked to hear from you. It also does not decide your pricing or promise results. You own the offer, the consent rules and the sales conversation.

1. Start with the interview

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT. It asks you questions one at a time, then writes a profile of your business that every later step uses.

Interview prompt
You are setting up an automation called **Speed to lead: reply to every new lead in under a minute, qualify and book them** for my business. Before building anything, interview me so the automation fits my business exactly.

How to interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Keep each question short and plain.
- When I'm vague, ask a follow-up with 2-4 example answers I can pick from.
- When I say "skip" or "not sure", use the safest default, mark it `(default)`, and move on.
- Never ask me for a password, card number, API key or code. When a login or key is needed, write down where it will be needed and that I'll add it myself.
- About every 6 questions, tell me how many are left.
- Stop after at most 25 questions, or sooner once you have what you need.

Cover these, in this order:
1. **The business.** What it sells, to whom, where, and how big it is (people, rough monthly customers or sales).
2. **The goal.** What this automation should achieve in 30 days, and the one number that proves it worked.
3. **Today.** Who does this job now, how, how long it takes a week, and what goes wrong.
4. **Voice.** How we talk to customers. Ask for 2-3 real examples (a post, an email, a reply) and words or claims we never use.
5. **Tools and accounts.** Which apps I use for email, calendar, CRM, payments, social, website and files. Who owns each login. Whether 2-factor codes go to my phone.
6. **Rules.** Things that must always or never happen: legal, safety, refunds, prices, medical or financial claims, and when a human must step in.
7. **Approvals.** What can go out on its own from day 1, and what I want to approve first (and for how long).
8. **Data.** Where the facts come from (price list, FAQ, product data, past posts), and where results should be saved.
9. **Measuring.** Where the numbers live (analytics, CRM, bank) and whether I can give read access.
10. **Schedule and budget.** When it should run, quiet hours, and the monthly tool budget.
11. **WORKFLOW QUESTIONS:** 
   - Where do your leads come from today? List each source (website form, Facebook/Instagram lead ads, Google ads, DMs, phone, marketplace) and roughly how many a week from each.
   - What is the first reply you would send if you had all day? Paste 2 or 3 real replies that worked well.
   - What 3 to 5 questions tell you whether a lead is worth a call? (Budget, timeline, location, size, what they need.) What answers make a lead a bad fit?
   - What counts as a hot, warm and cold lead for you? What should happen to each?
   - What do you want the lead to do next: book a call, get a quote, visit, or buy? Which calendar or booking link do they use, and which days and hours are open?
   - Which channels may we use to reply: email, SMS, DM, WhatsApp? Do your forms record permission to text or email? What is your opt-out wording?
   - What hours may messages go out? What should happen to a lead that comes in at night?
   - If a lead doesn't answer, how many follow-ups are okay, how far apart, and when do we stop?
   - Which CRM or sheet should every lead and conversation be logged in? What fields do you already use (stage, source, owner, notes)?
   - Who gets alerted for hot leads, and by what (text, email, Slack)? Who takes over, and how fast must they respond?

When done, write `profile.md` with these headings, filled from my answers:
`Business` · `Goal and success number` · `Current process and pain` · `Voice (with examples)` · `Never say or do` · `Tools and who owns each login` · `Needs human approval` · `Facts and data sources` · `Where results go` · `Analytics access` · `Schedule` · `Budget` · `Workflow settings` · `Open questions`.
Then list the setup steps only I can do (logins, keys, payments), in order, each with a time estimate.

Models

  • Classify the lead and spot spam or duplicates: A small, cheap model. Simple labeling, run on every lead, so cost and speed matter.
  • Write the first reply and qualifying questions: A mid-tier chat model such as GPT-4o-class or Claude Sonnet-class. It has to sound human and follow your rules, and it must answer within seconds.
  • Score the lead and decide next step: Same mid-tier model, with a strict JSON output. Structured output makes it easy for the automation to route the lead.
  • Weekly review and message improvements: Your strongest model (Claude or ChatGPT). Runs once a week, reads the results and proposes changes. Quality beats cost here.

3. Setup

  1. YouRun the interview prompt and save profile.md. Check the qualifying questions, hot/warm/cold rules and the 'never say' list carefully.30 min
  2. YouCreate or confirm accounts: automation tool (n8n or Make), calendar/booking tool, CRM or Google Sheet, an email or SMS sender, and ManyChat if you use DMs. Pay and accept terms yourself; keep keys in each tool's credential store.45 min
  3. YouSet up your consent and opt-out: add permission wording to forms, make sure SMS has an opt-out line, and decide sending hours. If unsure about the rules where you operate, ask a professional.20 min
  4. AICreate the CRM sheet or fields: lead id, received time, source, name, contact, message, score, status, first reply time, next action, owner, notes.15 min
  5. AIBuild the intake scenario: a webhook per lead source writes the lead to the sheet and starts the flow. Send a test lead to confirm it lands.45 min
  6. AIBuild the reply step: the AI writes the first reply from the lead's own words and profile.md, a rules check runs, and the message is sent.45 min
  7. AIBuild the qualify and book path: AI reads replies, scores the lead, offers the booking link or slots, and updates the CRM.60 min
  8. AIBuild the follow-up path: a timer checks for no reply and sends up to the allowed number of follow-ups, then stops and tags the lead cold.30 min
  9. YouTest with 10 fake leads (good fit, bad fit, spam, angry, duplicate, wrong language, night-time). In shadow mode, have the AI draft but send to you first for the first 20 real leads.1 to 2 hours, then about a week
  10. YouSwitch to auto-send for the lead types that were clean in shadow mode. Keep hold-for-human on for everything else.10 min

4. How it runs

  1. A lead arrivesWebhook from the form, ad lead form, or ManyChat

    Each source sends the lead to one webhook in your automation tool: name, contact details, message, source and time. DM leads arrive from ManyChat after the person comments a keyword or sends a message.

  2. Clean and checkAutomation tool + small AI model

    Check the contact is valid, look for duplicates in the CRM, drop obvious spam, and note whether you have permission to message them on that channel. A duplicate updates the existing record instead of starting a second conversation.

  3. Log the lead right awayCRM or Google Sheets

    Create the record with the received time so you can measure how long the first reply takes.

  4. Alert a human if hotSMS, email or Slack to you

    If the lead hits a hot rule (big budget, urgent timeline, named referral), text you at once. The AI still replies; you can jump in.

  5. Write the first replyMid-tier AI model

    The AI answers in your voice, mentions what the person actually asked about, asks one low-friction question, and offers the booking link if they are clearly ready. It never invents prices, availability or promises.

  6. Run the checks, then sendAutomation tool

    Rule checks run on the draft (see QA). If it passes and the time is within sending hours, it goes out by the best channel within about a minute. Otherwise it is held for you, or queued to the start of sending hours.

  7. Qualify by conversationAI model with your questions

    When they reply, the AI asks your qualifying questions one at a time, answers simple questions from your FAQ, and updates the CRM fields from the answers.

  8. Score and routeAI model with JSON output

    The AI scores the lead hot, warm, cold or not a fit using your rules. Hot and warm get the booking offer, cold goes to a slow nurture, not a fit gets a polite close.

  9. Book the callCal.com, Calendly or GoHighLevel calendar

    The lead picks a slot from the real calendar. They get a confirmation and a reminder, and you get the lead summary before the call.

  10. Follow up if they go quietTimer in the automation tool

    If there is no reply, send follow-ups at the spacing you chose (for example after a few hours, one day, three days). Each is short, different, and gives an easy way to say no. After the last one the lead is tagged cold and stops receiving messages.

  11. Log everythingCRM or Google Sheets

    Every message, score, booking and status change is written back so you can see the full story of a lead and measure speed and conversion.

5. The prompts

First reply writerAI node right after the lead is logged. Fill {BRACKETS} from profile.md; the lead data is injected by the automation.
You write the first reply to a new lead for {BUSINESS NAME}, which {WHAT WE DO} for {WHO WE SERVE}. Our voice: {VOICE NOTES}. Here are two real examples of replies we like:
{EXAMPLE 1}
{EXAMPLE 2}

The lead came from {SOURCE} and wrote: "{LEAD MESSAGE}". Their name is {NAME}. The channel is {CHANNEL} (if SMS or DM: under 300 characters, no links except our booking link; if email: under 100 words).

Write one reply that: greets them by first name; refers to something specific they said; asks exactly ONE easy question from this list that fits best: {QUALIFYING QUESTION 1}, {QUALIFYING QUESTION 2}; and, only if they clearly want to talk, offers this booking link: {LINK}.

Never: promise prices, discounts, results or dates; claim to be a human or deny being an AI if asked; mention other customers; pressure them; use more than one question; use words from this list: {BANNED WORDS}. If the lead message is angry, about a legal, safety or medical issue, a refund, or you cannot tell what they want, return exactly HOLD_FOR_HUMAN and a one-line reason instead of a reply. Return only the reply text or HOLD_FOR_HUMAN.
Qualify and scoreAI node every time a lead replies. Returns JSON the automation can route on.
You are qualifying a lead for {BUSINESS NAME}. Our ideal customer: {IDEAL CUSTOMER}. Hot means: {HOT RULES}. Warm means: {WARM RULES}. Not a fit means: {NOT A FIT RULES}.

Here is the whole conversation so far and the fields we already know:
{CONVERSATION}
{KNOWN FIELDS}

Return JSON only with: "score" (hot, warm, cold, not_a_fit, unknown); "fields" (object with any of: budget, timeline, location, need, company_size, decision_maker; use null if not stated, never guess); "next_action" (ask_question, offer_booking, send_nurture, close_politely, hold_for_human); "next_question" (the single best next qualifying question we have not asked yet, or null); "reason" (max 20 words, using only what the lead said). If the lead asked something we cannot answer from the FAQ below, set next_action to hold_for_human.
FAQ:
{FAQ}
Follow-up writerAI node on each timer step when a lead has not replied.
A lead named {NAME} has not replied to our last message. This is follow-up number {N} of {MAX}. Here is the conversation: {CONVERSATION}. Channel: {CHANNEL}.

Write a short follow-up (under 40 words for SMS or DM, under 70 for email) in our voice: {VOICE NOTES}. It must be different from the earlier messages, add one small piece of value or one simple yes-or-no question, and make it easy to say no thanks. If this is the last follow-up, say it is the last one and that they can message us any time. Never use guilt, fake urgency or fake scarcity. Never repeat the booking link more than once in the whole sequence. Return only the message.
Pre-send message checkA second, separate AI node that reviews any draft before it is sent. A fail means hold for a human.
You are a strict reviewer. Here is a draft message to a lead and our rules: {RULES AND BANNED CLAIMS}. Here is what the lead actually said: {LEAD MESSAGE}. Check the draft for: any price, date, discount, guarantee or result we did not approve; any claim not supported by our FAQ; a second question; wrong name; wrong channel length; pressure or fake urgency; pretending to be human; anything unclear. Return JSON only: {"pass": true or false, "problems": [list of short strings]}. Be strict. When in doubt, fail it.
Weekly speed-to-lead reviewRun once a week on the CRM log. This is the learning loop.
Here is this week's lead log from our CRM (source, received time, first reply time, score, replies, booked yes/no, status, whether a human stepped in) and the messages we sent. Produce: 1) median and slowest first reply time; 2) reply rate, qualified rate and booking rate by source and by first-message style; 3) the 3 first messages and 3 follow-ups that got the best replies and the 3 that got the worst, with why you think so; 4) leads that were mis-scored (a 'cold' lead who later booked, a 'hot' lead who was not a fit); 5) three specific proposed edits to our prompts or rules, each as old text, new text, and the evidence. Do not apply anything. Mark each as SAFE (wording or order of questions) or NEEDS OWNER (changes to scoring rules, offer, claims, sending hours, number of follow-ups). Do not draw conclusions from fewer than 20 leads in a group; say 'not enough data' instead.

6. Quality checks

CheckIf it fails
Permission and opt-out check: no SMS or marketing email goes to anyone without recorded permission, and every SMS and email has a simple opt-out. A STOP or unsubscribe reply ends all messages at once.Do not send. Tag the lead 'no permission' and alert the owner. Never message an opted-out contact.
Claim check: a second AI pass plus simple text rules block any price, discount, date, guarantee or result that is not in the approved list.Hold for a human with the draft attached. Do not auto-fix and send.
Duplicate and loop check: only one active conversation per contact, a maximum number of messages per day, and no reply to your own automated emails or out-of-office replies.Pause that lead and flag it in the sheet.
Sending-hours check: messages outside your allowed hours (in the lead's time zone if known) are queued, not sent.Queue until the start of the next window. Hot leads still alert you immediately.
Booking check: only count a lead as booked when the calendar returns a confirmed event, and check for double-booking.Mark 'booking unconfirmed' and send the owner a task to contact the lead.
Hold for a human: angry or upset leads, refund, legal, medical or safety topics, a question outside the FAQ, a request for a person, or anything the AI scores 'unknown' twice.Stop all automation on that lead, alert you with the full conversation, and send nothing until you reply.
Test-and-log check: every lead must have a CRM record with received time, first reply time and source.Retry the logging step; if it still fails, alert the owner. A lead is never allowed to exist without a record.

7. Learning loop

Reads: The CRM log: source, time received, time of first reply, whether they replied, score, booked or not, who stepped in. Plus the messages themselves and any 'unsubscribe' or complaint replies.

When: A quick look every morning at yesterday's speed and held leads. Full review every Friday. Fixed checkpoints at day 7, day 30 and day 90, comparing the same measures each time.

Compares: First reply time against the one-minute goal. Reply rate, qualified rate and booking rate by lead source, by message style, and by time of day. Only groups of 20 or more leads are compared; smaller ones are marked 'not enough data'.

Changes on its own: Without asking: the wording and order of follow-up messages, and which of your approved qualifying questions come first, within the voice rules you gave. It records each change in learnings.md with the evidence. Every other change is proposed to you.

Guardrails: It cannot change the offer, price talk, claims, scoring rules, sending hours, the number of follow-ups, who gets contacted, or any consent and opt-out rule. It cannot loosen a hold-for-human rule. It never changes more than one thing at a time per source, so results stay readable, and it keeps the previous version for a one-step rollback.

Weekly

  • Run the weekly review, approve the SAFE edits and decide on the NEEDS OWNER ones.30 min
  • Send yourself a fake lead from each source to check the whole path still works.10 min
  • Clean up the CRM: merge duplicates, close stale leads, review opt-outs.15 min

Monthly

  • Check bills and limits: automation operations, SMS credits, AI usage, ManyChat or CRM plan. Set alerts before limits pause your workflows.15 min
  • Re-authorize any expired connection (Google, Meta/Instagram, calendar) and review the month's numbers against the goal in profile.md.20 min
  • Re-read your qualifying questions, hot/warm/cold rules and FAQ, and update them for new offers or prices.20 min

9. What goes wrong (and the fix)

ProblemFix
A lead source sends a different data shape (nested form fields, empty values), so the webhook breaks or the AI sees a blank message.Run each source once with 'Run once' or a test event, check the exact fields in the log, and add a rule that an empty message gets a generic safe reply or is held.
The first message is a long, pushy pitch, so people ignore it.Keep it short, personal and one easy yes/no style question; test two styles and compare after 20 or more leads each.
The AI has too little business information and gives vague or wrong answers.Give it your website text, FAQ and price rules in the prompt, mark what is unknown, and hold anything outside it.
Messages go out without consent, or without a way to opt out.Record consent on forms, include opt-out lines, honor STOP at once, and ask a professional about the rules where you operate.
Instagram DM automation fails: the first DM must be set to send as a comment reply, and buttons do not show for some desktop users.Enable the setting, include a plain-text option ('reply with YES'), and match keyword variations and misspellings.
Infinite loops or the same lead enrolled several times in a workflow (for example a GoTo step without a limit).Set a maximum run count, use 'stop on reply', and check the enrollment history during tests.
Webhook returns an immediate success while the real work failed, or ManyChat sends an invalid JSON test.Respond only after the key steps finish, test with a real selected contact, and don't edit the first and last nodes unless you know what they do.
Connections expire or you hit your monthly operations or credit limit, which silently pauses everything.Set usage alerts, put the monthly re-check in your calendar, and have the automation text you when a run errors.
Leads are mixed between businesses or clients in one account and answers cross-contaminate.Use a separate sub-account or workspace for each business.
The scoring is wrong, so good leads get ignored or bad ones eat your time.Check mis-scored leads in the weekly review and adjust the rules with your approval. Review a sample of 'cold' leads each week.
