One Video, Every Platform

Every time you finish one video, it is cut into clips and turned into ready-to-post text for each platform you use, checked, and scheduled, with you approving the first batches.

Set upAbout 3 to 5 hours the first time, plus 1 week of approving every batch.DifficultymediumFrom15 videos
Untested means

This workflow is built from what the videos in our study show working. We haven’t run it on our own business yet, so it’s free. When we’ve tested it, the tested version (with our results and fixes) becomes paid. General information, not legal, tax or financial advice: check the rules where you operate.

What it covers

Repurpose one piece of content for every platform, Cut long videos into short clips, Add captions and subtitles, Write titles, descriptions and hashtags, Write posts, captions and threads, Schedule and publish posts, Make graphics, carousels and images.

Doesn’t cover: Making the original video, the platform logins and 2-factor codes, deciding which clips are on-brand for sensitive topics, replying to comments, paid ads, and rights to anyone else's footage. It will not copy other creators' content for you, and it can't promise reach or views.

1. Start with the interview

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT. It asks you questions one at a time, then writes a profile of your business that every later step uses.

Interview prompt
You are setting up an automation called **One Video, Every Platform** for my business. Before building anything, interview me so the automation fits my business exactly.

How to interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Keep each question short and plain.
- When I'm vague, ask a follow-up with 2-4 example answers I can pick from.
- When I say "skip" or "not sure", use the safest default, mark it `(default)`, and move on.
- Never ask me for a password, card number, API key or code. When a login or key is needed, write down where it will be needed and that I'll add it myself.
- About every 6 questions, tell me how many are left.
- Stop after at most 25 questions, or sooner once you have what you need.

Cover these, in this order:
1. **The business.** What it sells, to whom, where, and how big it is (people, rough monthly customers or sales).
2. **The goal.** What this automation should achieve in 30 days, and the one number that proves it worked.
3. **Today.** Who does this job now, how, how long it takes a week, and what goes wrong.
4. **Voice.** How we talk to customers. Ask for 2-3 real examples (a post, an email, a reply) and words or claims we never use.
5. **Tools and accounts.** Which apps I use for email, calendar, CRM, payments, social, website and files. Who owns each login. Whether 2-factor codes go to my phone.
6. **Rules.** Things that must always or never happen: legal, safety, refunds, prices, medical or financial claims, and when a human must step in.
7. **Approvals.** What can go out on its own from day 1, and what I want to approve first (and for how long).
8. **Data.** Where the facts come from (price list, FAQ, product data, past posts), and where results should be saved.
9. **Measuring.** Where the numbers live (analytics, CRM, bank) and whether I can give read access.
10. **Schedule and budget.** When it should run, quiet hours, and the monthly tool budget.
11. **WORKFLOW QUESTIONS:** 
   - What kind of videos do you make (talks, tutorials, interviews, product demos) and about how long are they?
   - How often do you finish a new video, and where does it get saved?
   - Which platforms do you want posts for: YouTube, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, others? Which are the top two?
   - How many clips per video do you want, and what length range for each?
   - Do you want captions burned into the clips? What style (big, small, brand color)?
   - What should every post point to: a website, a booking page, an app, a newsletter? Give the exact link and how each platform handles links.
   - Do your videos include other people, customers, music or footage you don't own? What are the rules for using them?
   - Which hashtags, tags or keywords do you already use, and which topics are banned?
   - What are the best times to post for your audience, if you know, and how many posts per day per platform is too many?
   - Who approves posts at first, and what should always need a human (testimonials, prices, health or legal claims)?

When done, write `profile.md` with these headings, filled from my answers:
`Business` · `Goal and success number` · `Current process and pain` · `Voice (with examples)` · `Never say or do` · `Tools and who owns each login` · `Needs human approval` · `Facts and data sources` · `Where results go` · `Analytics access` · `Schedule` · `Budget` · `Workflow settings` · `Open questions`.
Then list the setup steps only I can do (logins, keys, payments), in order, each with a time estimate.

Models

  • Transcription with word timestamps: Whisper-style speech-to-text. Clip cutting and captions both need exact word times.
  • Choosing clips: A strong reasoning model (Claude or ChatGPT). Finding a complete, self-contained moment with a strong first line is a judgment task.
  • Titles, captions, hashtags per platform: A strong writing model. Each platform needs different wording; quality here is what people read.
  • QA review: A fresh session of a strong model. Independent check on facts, claims and clip boundaries.
  • Cutting, captions, reframing: ffmpeg or an editor/clipping tool (CapCut, an AI clipper). Exact cuts are done by tools, not by a language model.
  • Sheet updates, file names, tagging: A cheap, fast model. Simple tasks.

3. Setup

  1. YouList your platforms and make sure every account exists, is verified, and (for Instagram/Facebook) is a business or creator account linked to a Page.30 to 60 min
  2. AIRun the interview and write profile.md (voice, platforms, links, banned topics, approval rules). Create folders: inbox/, transcripts/, clips/, posts/, and files queue.csv, posts.csv, learnings.md.20 min
  3. YouSign up for a posting tool that supports your platforms, or decide to use each platform's own scheduler. Connect the accounts yourself (logins and 2-factor codes).30 min
  4. YouCreate API keys (transcription, AI model, posting tool). Store them in the credential store or a .env file, never in chat. Note the monthly limit on each.30 min
  5. AIBuild the run: watch inbox/ for a new video, then steps 1 to 10 below. Run one video end to end with posting turned off.1 to 2 h
  6. YouReview the clips and text from the test. Fix voice, clip length, caption style and banned words until you would post them yourself.30 min
  7. AICreate the schedule and the 48-hour/7-day results task. Start in 'approve everything' mode.20 min
  8. YouApprove batches for one week. Move to 'approve only flagged items' after 5 clean batches in a row.10 min per video

4. How it runs

  1. New video arrivesFolder watch or a sheet row (status = new)

    You drop the finished video, or a link, into inbox/ (or add a row). The run starts on a schedule or on the new file.

  2. TranscribeWhisper-style speech-to-text

    Saves a transcript with word timestamps. Flags audio too quiet or too noisy to use.

  3. Find the best clipsAI

    Reads the transcript and picks complete moments of the length you set, each starting on a strong line and ending on a finished thought. Skips banned topics. Saves start/end times and a one-line reason.

  4. Cut and reframeffmpeg or clipping tool

    Cuts each clip, reframes for vertical (9:16) with the speaker centered, and keeps a landscape copy for platforms that want it.

  5. Burn in captionsEditor/clipping tool or ffmpeg

    Uses word timestamps for captions, keeps them out of the platform button zones, and checks they match the speech.

  6. Write text per platformAI

    For each clip and the full video: one search phrase, then a different title, caption and hashtag set per platform, in your voice, with the right link. Saves to posts.csv.

  7. Make extra posts (optional)AI + graphic tool

    Text-only posts for LinkedIn, X or a newsletter from the transcript, plus one simple quote graphic if you want it.

  8. Quality checksScript + AI review

    Runs the checks in section 7. Passing items become 'ready'; failing ones are fixed once, then held for you.

  9. Spread out and schedulePosting tool or platform schedulers

    Staggers posts: not all clips on the same day, not the same clip on every platform at the same minute. Respects your daily limits per platform. Marks each row 'scheduled', then 'posted' with the link.

  10. Measure and learnPlatform analytics + AI

    At 48 hours and 7 days, pulls numbers per post, compares to your own recent median, and writes lessons to learnings.md. Step 3 and step 6 read it next time.

Weekly learning reviewStep 10. After the 7-day checkpoint.
Read posts.csv: each row is one post with platform, clip length, hook type, topic, post time, caption style, and 48h and 7d numbers (views, watch time or percent watched, likes, comments, shares, clicks if known).

Compare within each platform only. For each tag, compare the median of posts with that tag to the median of all posts on that platform in the last 30 days. Only call it a pattern with at least 5 posts. Report: best platform for each clip type, hook types to use more or less, best length range, best posting windows. Propose ONE change for next week. Don't touch the profile rules. Write up to 5 lines to learnings.md, each with the date, evidence and change. With fewer than 10 posts on a platform, write 'not enough data' for it.

6. Quality checks

CheckIf it fails
Clip starts and ends on a full sentence; no cut words; no jump in the middle of a word.Adjust times by a fraction of a second and re-cut once; then hold.
Clip stands alone (a stranger understands it) and the first 3 seconds carry the hook.Pick another moment or hold.
Captions match the audio, are readable on a phone and stay out of the platform button zones.Re-render once; then hold.
Format per platform: aspect ratio, length limit, file size, resolution.Re-export in the right format.
Every claim in the text is supported by the clip; no testimonial, price, health, legal or financial claim unless approved in the profile.Hold for human.
Text differs across platforms, contains the search phrase where needed, uses the right link or 'link in bio' wording.Rewrite once.
Banned words, topics and private customer details.Hold for human. Never auto-post.
Rights: no third-party music, footage or faces without permission recorded in the profile.Hold for human.
Duplicate and spacing check: not the same clip already posted, not more than your daily limit per platform, no two near-identical posts at the same time. Repeated near-identical posts across accounts risk being flagged as duplicate content.Move to another slot or skip.
Hold-for-human rule: any failed check after one retry, any item marked sensitive, any account warning, or any spend over the limit stops that item and messages you.Pause that item; other items continue.

7. Learning loop

Reads: Per-post numbers from each platform (or the posting tool): views, watch time or percent watched, likes, comments, shares, clicks if tracked. Plus the tags saved with each post: platform, clip length, hook type, topic, caption style, post time.

When: Fixed checkpoints per post: 48 hours and 7 days. One weekly review on the same day each week.

Compares: Each post to the median of the last 30 days on the same platform. Tag groups against the rest. It needs 5 or more posts per tag and 10 per platform before it calls anything a pattern.

Changes on its own: On its own: how many clips to cut, preferred clip length, hook types and topics to pick first, hashtag sets, posting windows, and which platforms get the daily slots. One change per week.

Guardrails: One variable per week. It never changes your voice, banned list, links, approval rules or daily post limits. It never posts to a new platform on its own. Clicks and conversions count more than views when you track them. A post that gets a warning, strike or many negative comments pauses that topic and messages you. All changes are logged in learnings.md with evidence.

9. What goes wrong (and the fix)

ProblemFix
Poor source video gives poor clips and posts. Everything downstream is built on it.Only feed it finished videos you are proud of; the QA step checks the clip stands alone.
Posting fails from platform quotas or rate limits (for example TikTok or YouTube daily limits).Stay under the limits in your profile; check the posting tool's failed-posts list; mark failed rows, don't retry in a loop.
Expired social connections or page tokens.Reconnect monthly and after any password change; the run messages you when a post fails for auth.
Instagram can't take a direct file upload through some setups, or has format limits (one recipe used a workaround with a temporary image link).Use a posting tool that supports the format, or schedule that platform by hand.
A shared file isn't public, so the renderer can't read it.Set the sharing permission the tool needs, or upload directly through its API.
Sloppy JSON from the AI breaks the posting step.Ask for JSON only and validate it before posting; send invalid output back for one fix.
Wrong account IDs copied into the posting setup, so posts go to the wrong place.Test with one private or draft post per platform first.
Same content on every platform at the same time looks like spam and may be treated as duplicate content.Stagger posts and write different text per platform; nothing guarantees it avoids duplicate detection.
Clips cut mid-sentence or captions out of sync.Use word timestamps, re-cut once, hold if still wrong.
Unwarmed new accounts get flagged or banned (Instagram was called the most fragile in one recipe).Use real accounts you already run; post by hand for the first days of a new account.
Using other people's clips or music brings copyright risk.Only use your own footage and licensed audio; the profile records what is allowed.
A form or link that can publish to your accounts is open to anyone.Put basic login on any web form that triggers posting.

10. Proof from the study

Study task numbers (1,876 videos): repurposing 167 videos (37% run fully alone), clips 82 (54%), captions and subtitles 119 (48%), titles and hashtags 133 (18%), writing posts 241 (24%), scheduling posts 271 (73%), graphics 286 (48%). Top tools: ChatGPT, Claude, n8n, CapCut, Opus Clip. Cutting clips and scheduling are often fully automated; the wording of posts usually still gets a human read. 15 recipes were built from videos on this topic; several used a sheet + n8n/Make + a posting API, and one used Claude Code. Few had any learning from results. Income claims in clipping videos were not checked.
