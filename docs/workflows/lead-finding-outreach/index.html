Lead Finding and Outreach: find, personalize, send, follow up

Each week the AI builds a list of businesses or people that fit your ideal customer, finds their public business contact details, writes a short personal email or message for each, queues them for your approval, sends them slowly on a schedule with a few follow-ups, stops when someone replies or opts out, and hands you the replies.

Set upAbout 4 to 8 hours of work, spread over 2 to 3 weeks because new sending addresses must warm up before real sendingDifficultyhardFrom15 videos
Untested means

This workflow is built from what the videos in our study show working. We haven’t run it on our own business yet, so it’s free. When we’ve tested it, the tested version (with our results and fixes) becomes paid. General information, not legal, tax or financial advice: check the rules where you operate.

What it covers

Find leads and build prospect lists, Look up contact info and research leads, Write cold emails and DMs, Send outreach sequences, Follow up with leads who went quiet.

Doesn’t cover: Answering replies, taking calls, closing the sale, buying lists of people who never heard of you, and anything legal. You decide who you are allowed to contact in your country and what you offer. This is not legal advice. Cold calling and private-account social DMs are not covered.

1. Start with the interview

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT. It asks you questions one at a time, then writes a profile of your business that every later step uses.

Interview prompt
You are setting up an automation called **Lead Finding and Outreach: find, personalize, send, follow up** for my business. Before building anything, interview me so the automation fits my business exactly.

How to interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Keep each question short and plain.
- When I'm vague, ask a follow-up with 2-4 example answers I can pick from.
- When I say "skip" or "not sure", use the safest default, mark it `(default)`, and move on.
- Never ask me for a password, card number, API key or code. When a login or key is needed, write down where it will be needed and that I'll add it myself.
- About every 6 questions, tell me how many are left.
- Stop after at most 25 questions, or sooner once you have what you need.

Cover these, in this order:
1. **The business.** What it sells, to whom, where, and how big it is (people, rough monthly customers or sales).
2. **The goal.** What this automation should achieve in 30 days, and the one number that proves it worked.
3. **Today.** Who does this job now, how, how long it takes a week, and what goes wrong.
4. **Voice.** How we talk to customers. Ask for 2-3 real examples (a post, an email, a reply) and words or claims we never use.
5. **Tools and accounts.** Which apps I use for email, calendar, CRM, payments, social, website and files. Who owns each login. Whether 2-factor codes go to my phone.
6. **Rules.** Things that must always or never happen: legal, safety, refunds, prices, medical or financial claims, and when a human must step in.
7. **Approvals.** What can go out on its own from day 1, and what I want to approve first (and for how long).
8. **Data.** Where the facts come from (price list, FAQ, product data, past posts), and where results should be saved.
9. **Measuring.** Where the numbers live (analytics, CRM, bank) and whether I can give read access.
10. **Schedule and budget.** When it should run, quiet hours, and the monthly tool budget.
11. **WORKFLOW QUESTIONS:** 
   - Who is your ideal customer? Describe the type of business or person, their size, their location, and the problem you solve for them. Give 2 to 3 real examples of good customers.
   - What exactly are you offering, and what is the one small next step you want from a reply (a quick call, a yes/no, a free sample)?
   - Where are you based, and in which countries will you send? (Rules differ: the US, Canada and the EU are not the same.)
   - What is your business name and physical mailing address (a street address or registered PO box)? It must appear in every commercial email.
   - Which email address will you send from? Do you have a separate domain for cold email, or only your main business domain? Who controls the domain's DNS settings?
   - How many emails a day are you comfortable sending at the start? (The safe default is 20 to 30 a day per address, rising slowly.)
   - What are your proof points: real results, real customers you can name, real numbers? Anything not on this list must never be claimed.
   - Who must NOT be contacted: current customers, past complainers, competitors, partners, people who said no, or certain industries?
   - Do you want to approve every email before it goes, or only a sample? (Default: you approve everything for the first 100 emails.)
   - Who handles replies, how fast, and what should happen with an interested reply, a 'not now', a question, and an angry one?

When done, write `profile.md` with these headings, filled from my answers:
`Business` · `Goal and success number` · `Current process and pain` · `Voice (with examples)` · `Never say or do` · `Tools and who owns each login` · `Needs human approval` · `Facts and data sources` · `Where results go` · `Analytics access` · `Schedule` · `Budget` · `Workflow settings` · `Open questions`.
Then list the setup steps only I can do (logins, keys, payments), in order, each with a time estimate.

Models

  • Deciding if a lead fits (ICP check): A small, cheap model (Claude Haiku or GPT mini class). Simple yes/no with a reason, run on hundreds of leads.
  • Researching one lead and finding a real hook: A strong model with web or page-reading access. Personalization is only good if the fact it uses is real.
  • Writing the email and follow-ups: A strong model (Claude Sonnet or GPT-4o class). Tone and brevity decide whether anyone replies.
  • Second-opinion check on drafts: The same strong model with a checker prompt. Catches wrong facts, banned phrases and anything over the line before you see it.

  3. YouBuy one or two separate sending domains (similar to your brand, not your main domain) and create a mailbox on each. Keep your main business domain for real conversations only. Add the DNS records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) your email provider gives you.1 to 2 hours
  4. AICheck the DNS records with a free mail-test tool and tell you what is missing.10 min
  5. YouTurn on warm-up for each new mailbox (built into most cold email tools). Expect at least 2 weeks of warm-up before real sending, and send only a few emails a day at first.15 min, then 2 weeks of waiting

4. How it runs

  1. Define who fitsprofile.md

    The ideal customer, the places you will look (maps, directories, job posts, LinkedIn-style searches, your own website visitors), and the do-not-contact list.

  2. Find leadsApollo, Apify actor, Google Maps scraper, or Google search + AI cleanup

    Pull a batch (start with 50). Keep the source link for each. Remove duplicates against the sheet and against past contacts. Respect the sites' terms and the platforms' limits: do not scrape logged-in private data.

  3. Check fitSmall model

    For each lead: fits, maybe, or no, with one sentence why. Only 'fits' moves on. Keep the reason in the sheet.

  4. Find a work contactEmail finder/verifier (for example Apollo, AnyMailFinder, a verifier such as MillionVerifier)

    Look for a role-based or named business email from public or licensed sources. Verify it. Drop invalid, risky or catch-all results. Never guess an address and send.

  5. Research one real hookStrong model + page reading

    Read the lead's own website or public post and find one true, specific thing worth mentioning, with its source link. No hook found: use a plain non-personal email or skip the lead.

  6. Write the emailStrong model

    Short first email (about 80 to 100 words), plain text, one clear ask, no tracking pixels or heavy formatting. Then 2 follow-ups with new, useful content (not just 'bumping this').

  7. Check the draftChecker prompt + script

    Run the compliance and quality checks in section 7 (facts, banned phrases, length, address, opt-out line). Failed drafts are fixed once automatically, then held.

  8. You approveGoogle Sheet or the email tool's draft view

    You read a batch, mark approve or edit. During the first 100 emails you read every one; later you read a sample plus everything the checker flagged.

  9. Send slowlyCold email tool

    Send only in business hours for the lead's time zone, at your daily limit, spread out. Follow-ups after a few days. Stop-on-reply on. Open tracking off. Unsubscribe link or reply-to-opt-out line in every email.

  10. Handle replies and opt-outsCold email tool inbox + suppression list

    Any 'stop', 'unsubscribe' or 'remove me' goes on the Suppression tab immediately and is never contacted again, from any campaign. Interested replies are sent to you right away. The AI may draft a reply but never sends it.

  11. ReportWeekly message

    Sent, bounced, replied, positive replies, opt-outs, spam complaints, and which hook types worked.

5. The prompts

Fit check promptStep 3. Runs on every lead.
You decide if a business is a good lead for the offer below.

Offer: {one sentence from profile.md}
Ideal customer: {from profile.md}
Do-not-contact rules: {from profile.md}

Lead data: {name, website text or description, location, size if known}

Answer JSON only: {"fit": "yes|maybe|no", "reason": "one plain sentence that quotes something from the lead data", "missing": "what you could not tell"}
If the lead data is thin, answer maybe. Do not guess facts.
Hook research promptStep 5. One lead at a time, with page-reading access.
Read the lead's own website and any public post I give you. Find ONE specific, true thing that connects to my offer (a new service, a hiring post, a recent project, a product line). Give me the fact, the exact words or page where you saw it, and the link.

Rules: only use what you can see on the page. If you find nothing specific, say 'no hook' instead of inventing one. Do not mention anything private, personal or family-related. Do not use flattery without a fact behind it.

My offer: {offer}
Lead: {name, website}
First email promptStep 6. Writes the first message and two follow-ups.
Write a cold email from {sender name} at {business} to {first name} at {company}.

Facts you may use: the hook below (with its source) and the proof points from my profile. Do not add any other claim, number or customer name.
Hook: {hook}
Proof points: {proof points}

Rules:
- 80 to 100 words, plain text, no bold, no links in the first email except what profile.md allows.
- Subject under 40 characters, honest, describes what the email is about. No fake 'Re:' or 'Fwd:'.
- Sound like a person: one idea, one clear ask that is easy to answer with yes or no.
- Never write: 'I hope this finds you well', 'I came across your profile', 'game-changer', 'revolutionary', guaranteed results, fake urgency, or praise that is not tied to the hook.
- Do not say or imply we know them if we do not.
- End with the sender's name, business, and this exact line: {postal address line}. Then: {opt-out line from profile.md}.

Then write follow-up 1 (sent after 3 to 4 days, adds one new useful point, 40 to 60 words) and follow-up 2 (sent after 7 days, a polite last note with an easy out, 30 to 50 words). Return JSON: {subject, body, follow_up_1, follow_up_2}.
Short direct message promptFor a business social account or contact form, only where that platform allows it and you send by hand.
Write a 2 to 3 sentence message from {sender} to {first name}. Mention this one true thing: {hook}. Ask one easy yes/no question related to {offer}. No links, no hashtags, no emojis, no pressure. It must read like a normal person wrote it. If the message would break the platform's rules for unsolicited messages, say so instead of writing it.
Draft checker promptStep 7. A separate reviewer, ideally a different run from the writer.
You are a strict reviewer. Check this email against the facts and rules, and answer JSON: {"pass": true/false, "problems": [list]}.

Facts allowed: {hook with source, proof points}
Rules: every claim in the email must appear in the allowed facts; no guaranteed results; no fake urgency; no misleading subject or fake reply/forward; sender name and business are real; the postal address line and opt-out line are present; length 80 to 100 words (follow-ups shorter); none of these phrases appear: {banned list}.
Also flag: anything that sounds like praise without a fact, anything that could be taken as a threat, and any medical, legal or financial promise.

Email:
{subject and body}
Reply sorter promptStep 10. Reads each reply and tells you what to do. It never answers for you.
Classify this reply to my cold email as exactly one of: interested, question, not_now, not_interested, opt_out, out_of_office, bounce, angry, other. If the sender asks to stop, be removed, or says it is unwanted in any way, choose opt_out. Return JSON: {"label": "...", "one_line_summary": "...", "suggested_next_step": "..."}. If label is angry or you are unsure, say 'human only'.

Every fact in the email is on the allowed list with a source link (the hook and your proof points)Rewrite once; if it still fails, hold for a human.
Email has a truthful sender name and subject, your physical postal address, and a clear way to opt outHold for a human. Nothing goes out without all three.
No banned phrases, no guaranteed results, no fake urgency, no fake 'Re:' or 'Fwd:' subject, no medical, legal or financial promisesRewrite once; if it still fails, hold for a human.

7. Learning loop

Reads: The cold email tool's numbers (sent, bounced, replied, positive replies, opt-outs, spam complaints) and the reply labels from the reply sorter, pulled into the sheet. Also which lead source, which hook type and which subject line each lead came from.

When: After every 100 emails sent, and again at fixed checkpoints: 7 days and 14 days after each batch (replies mostly arrive within days). A monthly review of the whole month.

Compares: Batches against each other with one thing changed at a time (for example hook about a hiring post vs a new service, or subject line A vs B), on similar sizes of at least 100 emails each. Judge on positive replies per 100 sent, not opens (open tracking is off).

Changes on its own: On its own it may reorder which lead sources and hook types to try more, choose the better subject line for the next batch, and tune send time. It writes proposed wording changes to learnings.md but does not change the email prompt until you approve.

Guardrails: It never raises the daily limit by more than you allowed, never adds new sending addresses or domains, never removes the opt-out line, address or checks, never contacts anyone on Suppression, and pauses all sending if bounces or complaints pass the limits. Every change and the reason is logged.

9. What goes wrong (and the fix)

ProblemFix
Sending from your main business domain, or before the mailboxes are warmed up, lands emails in spam and can get the domain flaggedUse separate sending domains, warm up for at least 2 weeks, and start with a low daily number.
Scrapers miss emails, return duplicates, or break when a site changesTest on a small batch first, dedupe against the sheet, verify every email, and expect to repair the scraper from time to time.
The AI invents a personal detail, or the 'personalized' line is wrong or awkward (for example generic flattery, odd phrasing)The hook must carry a source link, the checker compares every claim to the allowed facts, and you read the first 100 drafts.
Too many messages too fast get your email account or a social account restricted (LinkedIn-type limits were raised in the recipes)Keep a low daily cap, use the tool's schedule, and send social messages by hand within the platform's limits.
Someone opted out but is emailed again from another campaign or an old listOne shared Suppression list checked before every send in every campaign. Opt-outs are honored promptly; the US rule allows up to 10 business days, but do it the same day.
The same person or company is contacted twiceDedupe against the sheet on email and company domain before every batch.
Costs rise quietly: long research runs, retries and lead-tool credits (one recipe noted that deep research per lead is slow and token-heavy)Set monthly spending caps, run research on small batches, and track cost per positive reply.
API keys or login details are pasted into prompts or notesKeep keys in the tool's vault or a .env file, never in chat or files you share, and rotate them if exposed.
Rules differ by country and the AI cannot know your local lawFill in the country rules in profile.md after checking them (or asking a lawyer), and skip countries you are unsure about.
